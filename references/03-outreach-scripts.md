# Stage 3 — Message Design (First Touch & Replies)

## Why generic first touches get ignored

A cold message must first answer the recipient's silent question: **"Is this person relevant to me, or spam?"** Common fatal patterns:

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| `Hi, I am {name} from {company}, a freight forwarder…` | Textbook mass-blast opener; reads as scam/spam, deleted unread |
| Leading with your service list / job title | Hard sell; buyers in target market are numb to this; can trigger block/report |
| No "how I found you" | Lacks the personal touch that signals you did homework |
| High-friction question (`What's your main product?`) | Asks the buyer to organize information for you |
| Promise of a quote with no reason to reply | No payoff for engaging |
| One long block of text | Long messages go unread |

## Optimizing principles

- Drop "Hi" and the `freight forwarder` self-label; open naturally with `{你的名字} here` or a warm, specific line.
- **Segment 1 = about the customer**: how you found them / what they deal in → proves you understand their market, establishes "not a mass message".
- **Segment 2 = about you, but in customer-benefit terms**: what they gain (door-to-door, clearance included, "others like you use this"), not a service checklist.
- **Segment 3 = low-friction question + free value**: a yes/no or two-option question, plus a free landed-cost/quote offer "even just for comparison".
- Keep each bubble to 1–2 sentences; send as 3 separate bubbles.
- Prepare 2–3 variants and mix them to avoid spam detection.
- Time it to the recipient's working hours. Convert from your local time (e.g. target UTC+3 → send 14:00–22:00 China time).

## Templates (fill placeholders)

### Template A — clear product category (most common)
1. `{你的名字} here — I was looking into {品类} importers in {城市} and came across your company.`
2. `I handle door-to-door shipping from {origin} to {城市} with clearance included — the same route most China importers here use.`
3. `Quick question: do you buy {品类} direct from China? Send me one item and I'll work out the landed cost to {城市} for you, free — useful even just to check against your current rate.`

### Template B — phones / electronics / digital
1. `{你的名字} here — saw you deal in {品牌/品类} here in {城市}, so I thought I'd reach out.`
2. `I ship from {origin} to {城市} door-to-door with clearance handled — a lot of phone and electronics importers here use us.`
3. `Do you source direct from China? Send me one model — I'll send you the landed cost to {城市} free, just for comparison.`

### Template C — general wholesaler / category unclear
1. `{你的名字} here — found your company while looking at general importers and wholesalers in {城市}.`
2. `I handle {origin}-to-{城市} shipping, FCL and LCL, door-to-door with clearance included.`
3. `What do you mainly import from China? Send me one item and I'll send a free landed-cost quote to {城市} — handy even just for a price check.`

Use conditional wording (`If you import from China…`) for weak-evidence leads; direct wording (`you import…`) for strong-evidence leads.

## Sending rhythm

- Mix variants A/B/C across the batch.
- Space bubbles ~10–20s; space leads ~3s+ (or more) to avoid rate limits.
- Send during the recipient's business hours.
- Follow-ups use three beats: light reminder → add value → new angle. Never just "Did you see my message?"

## Handling a reply (critical)

A reply is valuable — most cold leads never answer. **Read the customer's exact words and answer the question they actually asked.**

### Diagnose the intent
- A real reply is a positive signal even if it seems off-topic.
- Watch for a **role misunderstanding**: if the customer asks about your "printer" / product / catalogue, they may think you are a supplier, not a logistics provider. This is a clarification opportunity, not a sale.
- Classify intent (see `references/05-tracking-notes.md`): genuine inquiry = A, comparison/networking = B, auto-reply/curt = C, sent-no-reply = D, reject = E.

### Reply strategy
1. **Answer first, don't repeat your intro.** The customer already read your first touch; repeating "I'm {name}, I ship from…" is why replies go cold.
2. **Acknowledge their exact term** — reflect their word back so they feel heard.
3. **Correct misunderstanding gently**, don't argue. If they think you sell equipment: "Just to clarify — I'm on the shipping side, not the supplier."
4. **Bridge to your value**: "…but if you're importing X from China, I can get it shipped and cleared for you."
5. **End with a two-option question** to minimize reply effort: "Which do you need — the machine or the materials?" This is far better than an open "What do you need?".

### Case pattern (role misunderstanding)
Customer: *"I was enquiring about your printer."*

- ❌ Three bubbles re-introducing yourself and repeating the shipping pitch → read, no reply.
- ❌ Open question "What do you need?" → too much effort.
- ✅ **One short bubble**: "Ah, quick clarify — we're the shipping side, not a printer supplier :) But I can bring a printing machine or packaging stock in from China for you, cleared. Which do you need — the machine or the materials?"

Lesson: for replies, **one tight message beats a three-bubble burst**; correct + bridge + two-option question.

## Confirm before sending
Always show the final text to the user and get approval before any send. Messages are irreversible.
